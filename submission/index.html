The throat in your phone

A second of CD audio takes 1,411,200 bits, and an old landline used 64,000 for a second of voice. A mobile call usually gets about 12,000,1 which is far too few to send the sound of someone’s voice. So the caller’s phone listens in slices a fiftieth of a second long, works out what shape their throat and mouth were in for each slice, and sends only that. Your phone builds a throat to match and blows through it,2 and that is the voice you hear.

This is your voice sent the way secure government phones sent it in the 1980s. Phones today still send the same throat, with a smarter buzz.5

Every 20 ms the phone sends four things: the shape of the throat as ten numbers, the pitch of the voice, how loud it is, and one bit saying whether the sound was a buzz or a hiss. The other phone makes the buzz from clicks. A click is one sharp tick, standing in for each time the vocal folds snap shut, and it is repeated at the pitch of the voice. For a hiss it uses noise, which is how it says s, f and sh, and why a whisper still gets through.6 The phone only knows a tube of ten sections, and the drawing above is just a way to picture it.

Plate II. Why hold music sounds like that

Everything above assumes one throat and one voice. This is a piece of real elevator music7 going through the same phone.

Each packet has room for one pitch, but a chord is several notes at once. The phone picks the pitch that repeats most clearly and rebuilds every note as clicks at that one pitch.

Ten throat numbers can only describe a smooth outline with a few bumps. That is enough for the resonances of a mouth but nowhere near enough for a piano and a bass playing different notes, so what gets through is the overall brightness of the band.

When the phone can’t find one clear pitch at all, the packet goes as hiss, so drums and busy passages come out as noise shaped like the band. In this piece that is most of the packets, which is why the lower picture is mostly a blur.

A real phone today chooses its pulses to match the sound, so its hold music is less mushy than ours, but it is still built around one voice. Newer phones are starting to fix this.8
